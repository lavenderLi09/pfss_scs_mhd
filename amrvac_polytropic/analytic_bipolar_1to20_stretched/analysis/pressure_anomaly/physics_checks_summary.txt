what_was_tested
1) full-pole spherical shell, theta includes poles, AMRVAC pole treatment active
2) off-pole shell, theta trimmed away from poles, theta handled by user special boundary
3) two off-pole theta boundary variants were checked
   a) old working variant: zero-gradient hydro + extrapolated CT magnetic field + cell-centered analytical overwrite
   b) new analytical-continuation variant: theta ghost cells filled by Parker wind + analytical bipole continuation

background_and_reason
The original full-pole smoke test crashed immediately at iteration 0 with negative gas pressure near the south-pole ring.
To bypass the polar singularity and diagnose the problem, the theta domain was trimmed away from the exact poles.
This off-pole version ran stably, but then the question became whether the run is only numerically stable or also physically relaxing toward a reasonable solar-wind MHD state.
The later analytical-continuation theta boundary was introduced to test whether a more self-consistent ghost-cell state would reduce the off-pole boundary artifacts.

old_boundary_interpretation
The run was numerically stable for 50 steps.
However, it did not look like a clean Parker-like MHD relaxation.
Magnetic-field changes stayed small, density changed only mildly, but strong transverse velocities appeared near the off-pole theta cuts and local negative Vr appeared close to the inner boundary.
This suggested that the old off-pole theta boundary was numerically tolerable but not physically clean.

physical_judgement
1) full-pole run: not usable because it crashes immediately
2) off-pole old boundary: numerically stable, but still shows likely boundary-driven transverse flow and local inflow near the inner boundary
3) off-pole new analytical-continuation boundary: also numerically stable, but physically worse because it creates a strong pressure ring at the truncated theta caps on the first radial shell

working_hypothesis
The main unresolved issue is not just the cell-centered ghost state.
The stronger evidence points to the treatment of theta-boundary staggered magnetic faces under CT.
The later analytical-continuation attempt filled cell-centered ghost states more consistently, but the face-centered magnetic boundary still appears geometrically inconsistent enough to generate a pressure-compression ring near the theta cuts.

current_best_conclusion
At present, the off-pole shell is useful as a diagnostic configuration but cannot yet be interpreted as a clean, physically self-consistent solar-wind MHD relaxation.
The abnormal pressure behaviour is localized to the theta-cut boundaries and the first radial shell, and should be treated as a boundary artifact until the theta CT magnetic-face boundary is reworked.
