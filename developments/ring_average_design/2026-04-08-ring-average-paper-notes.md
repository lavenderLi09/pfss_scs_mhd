# Ring Average Paper Notes

## Source
- Local PDF read from [paper.pdf](/Users/zhaoyan/Documents/05_heliosphere/pfss_scs_mhd/amrvac_polytropic/ring_average_design/paper.pdf)
- Paper:
  - B. Zhang, K. A. Sorathia, J. G. Lyon, V. G. Merkin, M. Wiltberger
  - "Conservative averaging-reconstruction techniques (Ring Average) for 3-D finite-volume MHD solvers with axis singularity"
  - Journal of Computational Physics 376 (2019) 276-294
  - DOI: `10.1016/j.jcp.2018.08.020`

## What the paper is solving
- In cylindrical/spherical explicit finite-volume MHD, the azimuthal cells near the axis or pole become extremely thin.
- That makes the CFL timestep scale like the smallest azimuthal length, so in a `N x N x N`-type spherical mesh the timestep can effectively drop like `1/N^2` instead of `1/N`.
- Their goal is to remove this timestep bottleneck without changing:
  - the underlying curvilinear finite-volume solver
  - the mesh
  - the data structure

## Core idea
- They do **not** replace the original computational mesh.
- They introduce an **effective grid** near the pole/axis by grouping thin azimuthal cells into larger azimuthal chunks.
- After each solver update, they:
  - average conservative quantities within each chunk
  - reconstruct monotonically back to the original thin cells inside the chunk
- The physical grid remains the original grid, but the post-processed solution behaves as if small-scale azimuthal modes near the pole were filtered out.

## Fluid-variable algorithm
- This is a 1-D operation along azimuth inside each selected ring.
- Applied to conservative fluid variables:
  - mass
  - momentum
  - energy
- Reconstruction choices mentioned:
  - PCM
  - PLM
  - PPM
- In the paper they choose **PPM** as the main reconstruction, and state it gives up to 4th-order accuracy in the reconstruction step.

### Per-ring procedure
1. Pull one azimuthal ring into a 1-D array.
2. Partition it into chunks of `N_C` cells.
3. Compute the chunk average `rho_mean`.
4. Use standard PPM on the chunk averages to get left/right interface states `rho_L`, `rho_R`.
5. Build a parabola `A x^2 + B x + C` in each chunk, constrained by:
   - interface values
   - chunk-integrated conservation
6. Integrate that parabola piecewise over each original thin cell to recover reconstructed cell averages.

### Reconstruction formulas quoted in the paper
- Reconstructed density in local cell `k_l` within a chunk:

`rho_r(k_l) = A/(3 N_C^2) * (3 k_l^2 - 3 k_l + 1) + B/(2 N_C) * (2 k_l - 1) + C`

- With:

`A = 3 (rho_L - rho_R - 2 rho_mean)`

`B = 2 (3 rho_mean - rho_R - 2 rho_L)`

`C = rho_L`

### Important numerical meaning
- The chunk total is preserved exactly.
- Small azimuthal structure inside each chunk is removed or reduced.
- Because the reconstruction is monotone, the method avoids oscillations.
- In special cases like an exact step profile, the non-linear PPM reconstruction can reproduce the step sharply instead of behaving like a simple smooth low-pass filter.

## Magnetic-field algorithm

### Two cases
- If magnetic field is cell-centered and divergence cleaning is used:
  - treat magnetic variables like fluid variables
  - then apply divergence cleaning afterward
- If magnetic field is staggered / face-centered:
  - the method is more complicated
  - this is the part that matters most for CT-like solvers

### Why face-centered magnetic fields are harder
- You cannot independently reconstruct all three magnetic-flux components and still expect local `div B = 0`.
- Even if the total divergence of a chunk stays zero, each thin cell can violate `div B = 0` after naive reconstruction.

### Their solution
- Apply conservative averaging-reconstruction only to the first two face-normal components:
  - `Phi_i`
  - `Phi_j`
- Do **not** reconstruct `Phi_k` independently.
- Instead, update `Phi_k` through Faraday's law using **perturbation electric fields** `delta E_j` and `delta E_i` generated by the changes in `Phi_i` and `Phi_j`.

### Key formulas
- For reconstructed `Phi_i`, they define:

`delta E_j(k_l + 1) = sum_{k_l=1}^{N_C-1} (Phi_i^r(k_l) - Phi_i(k_l)) / delta t`

- For reconstructed `Phi_j`, they define:

`delta E_i(k_l + 1) = - sum_{k_l=1}^{N_C-1} (Phi_j^r(k_l) - Phi_j(k_l)) / delta t`

- They explicitly choose:

`delta E_k = 0`

### Why this preserves divergence
- Because `delta E_k = 0`, the perturbation electric field vanishes on chunk edges in the azimuthal direction.
- Then `delta Phi_k = 0` on chunk interfaces.
- This keeps total `div B` unchanged, and the paper states the update preserves zero magnetic divergence both locally and globally after reconstruction.

### Practical detail
- The `delta t` in these perturbation-electric-field formulas is only a numerical scale for the reconstruction update.
- In practice they just set `delta t = 1`.

## Chunk selection and effective CFL scale

### Chunk choice is not unique
- The paper explicitly says the chunk setup is problem- and resolution-dependent.
- Their simple design rule is to keep the effective radial-to-azimuthal aspect ratio near unity.

### Effective azimuthal length
- For uniform polar spacing, the effective azimuthal length at radial cell index `m` is written as:

`delta L_eff = ((2m - 1)/2) * (2 pi / N) * delta R`

- If the first cell is averaged into octants, its effective azimuthal length becomes about `pi/4 * delta R`, i.e. comparable to radial spacing.

### Chunking rule
- They propose choosing `2^k` cells per chunk such that:

`((2m - 1)/2) * pi * (2^k / N) ~= 1`

- So chunk size grows outward ring by ring.

### Example table from LFM
- For `N = 64`, the paper lists chunk counts equivalent to:
  - first ring: average over 8 cells
  - second ring: 16
  - third ring: 32
  - later rings eventually stop averaging

## How timestep is changed
- After Ring Average is applied, timestep is recomputed from the **effective grid**, not the original thin-cell grid.
- This is one of the most important points for our implementation choices.

### Their CFL viewpoint
- If chunking guarantees radial-to-azimuthal aspect ratio greater than one, timestep can be adjusted directly using the chunk grouping.
- More generally, they recompute CFL using:

`delta t = min( delta L_eff / V_max )`

- where:

`delta L_eff = delta V / min(A_i^*, A_j^*, A_k)`

- Here `A_i^*`, `A_j^*` are effective face areas on the effective chunk grid.

### Scaling result
- Main conclusion:
  - without Ring Average, timestep falls quadratically with resolution in the polar-limited case
  - with Ring Average, timestep falls linearly with resolution

## Axis/pole implementation details in the paper

### Boundary conditions
- Standard finite-volume pole/axis boundary conditions still apply.
- After Ring Average, boundary conditions must be refreshed so ghost cells are consistent with reconstructed values.

### Electric fields on the axis
- On staggered grids they set axial edge electric fields by azimuthal averaging:

`E_i(i, j=1, k) = (1/N) sum_k E_i(i, j=1, k)`

`E_k(i, j=1, k) = 0`

- This prevents numerical instability from noise generated on the axis.

### Axis loop damping
- They mention an additional damping fix for loop-like magnetic structures wrapped around the axis:

`delta E_i^axis = - A_fix * (1/N) sum_k Phi_k(k)`

- In LFM they often use `A_fix = 0.5`, or `1` for full removal.
- This is not the main Ring Average algorithm, but an auxiliary stabilization device for axis-centered loop buildup.

### Fourier reduction for vector fields
- A very important detail:
  - if vector components vary like `sin` or `cos` across the pole, monotone reconstruction can clip and distort them
  - especially for magnetic flux components
- Their fix is to remove the `m = 0, 1` Fourier components before Ring Average, apply Ring Average to the residual, then add those low modes back.
- This is a sophisticated part of the full method and is easy to miss if one only reads the abstract.

## Interpretation of the method

### It is not just a timestep trick
- The full paper method changes the near-pole state after each update.
- It is therefore an **algorithmic post-processing step on the solution**, not only a CFL modification.

### It is roughly a localized non-linear azimuthal filter
- In the discussion section, the authors explicitly say Ring Average is roughly equivalent to an aggressive monotonic filter in azimuth that removes high-`m` modes.
- But it is **not** identical to a simple Fourier or boxcar filter, because:
  - PPM is non-linear
  - step-like profiles may be preserved sharply
  - the filter response depends on chunk layout

### Their Fourier-space analysis
- For PCM they derive the averaged Fourier amplitude as:

`f_avg_tilde(m) = f_tilde(m) * (1/L) * [sin(L m delta) / sin(m delta)]^2`

- They interpret the averaging step as behaving like a double-boxcar-type filter in Fourier space.
- Higher-order reconstruction like PLM/PPM preserves low Fourier modes better than PCM.

## Validation results that matter for us
- They test:
  - field-loop advection
  - MHD blast waves
- The paper reports:
  - no serious spurious oscillations at the radial boundaries of the ring-average zone when CFL is adjusted consistently
  - solution sensitivity to chunk layout is modest
  - shock-profile differences between chunk layouts are on the order of a few percent near shocks
  - integrated plasma and magnetic energy are essentially unaffected in their tests

## Most important implications for AMRVAC

### What matches our current V1 plan
- Their motivation for larger `dt` is exactly the same as ours:
  - replace the limiting role of thin `phi` cells by a larger effective azimuthal scale near the pole

### What does **not** match our current V1 plan
- Their full method is **not** just a CFL change.
- They actually post-process the updated solution variables ring by ring.
- For staggered magnetic fields they introduce special perturbation-electric-field updates so that `div B = 0` is preserved.

### Practical conclusion
- A faithful paper-level implementation in AMRVAC would eventually have to touch:
  - post-update cell-centered conservative variables
  - face-centered magnetic fluxes if using CT/staggered fields
  - electric-field or CT-like update logic
  - ghost refresh after the post-processing
- So our current first target remains correctly scoped:
  - **CFL-only ring-average-inspired timestep relaxation**
  - not full conservative ring-chunk evolution

## Difference from our V1 in one sentence
- The paper's full Ring Average method changes both the allowed timestep and the near-pole updated state.
- Our V1 only changes the allowed timestep.
